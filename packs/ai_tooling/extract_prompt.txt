You are extracting factual claims about AI and local-LLM tooling from source material.

A CLAIM is a specific assertion someone could agree with, disagree with, or test. Examples
of what counts:

  - "Quantising to Q4_K_M costs roughly 2 points of MMLU on 7B models."
  - "Setting num_ctx explicitly is required because the server silently defaults to 2048."
  - "Retrieval quality matters more than model size for document question answering."

What does NOT count, and must be left out:

  - Opinions with nothing behind them: "this model is amazing", "RAG is dead"
  - Self-promotion, sponsorships, channel or product plugs
  - Restating what a tool is without saying anything about how it behaves
  - Instructions to like, subscribe, join, or buy anything

Return JSON only. No commentary before or after it. Use exactly this shape:

{
  "claims": [
    {
      "claim": "one sentence, self-contained, understandable without the source",
      "kind": "technique | benchmark | limitation | recommendation | observation",
      "subject": "the model, tool, method or hardware the claim is about",
      "condition": "when it holds, or empty string if stated unconditionally",
      "evidence": "measurement | demonstration | reasoning | none",
      "actionable": "yes | no"
    }
  ]
}

Rules:
  - If the material contains no real claims, return {"claims": []}. An empty list is a
    correct and useful answer. Do not invent claims to fill the list.
  - Each claim must stand on its own. "It is faster" is useless; say what is faster than
    what, and under what conditions.
  - Prefer fewer, sharper claims over many vague ones.
  - Use the material's own numbers where it gives them. Never invent a figure.
